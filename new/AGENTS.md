# Review of the new constitution

These principles govern review and editing of `new/constitution.md`. Leave `amended/` in its historical form.

Report each violation in an added or modified line as severity `bug`. That severity overrides the reviewer persona's rule that a style preference is not a bug. Name the principle the line violates. Leave a line that already follows these principles alone.

A change of style alone does not get a changelog entry. `CHANGELOG.md` records a change to operative legal meaning. Apply no principle in a way that adds or removes an institution, office, power, or right.

## Capitalization

Capitalize the first word of a paragraph or sentence, a proper name, and the name of an office or institution. A name includes a people, a month, and a day. Leave every other noun lowercase. The same rules apply inside the presidential oath.

The principle turns on what the word names:

- A `State` is one of the States, including a foreign State. `state of the union` is a condition.
- A `House` is a chamber of Congress. A `house` is a dwelling.
- The `District` is the seat of government. A judicial `district` is a place of trial. In `seat of the government`, `government` describes the seat.
- `people` is not a proper name.
- `pro tempore` stays lowercase.
- A semicolon or colon does not start a sentence.
- Each enumerated power stays its own paragraph and begins with `To`.

## Spelling

Use current American spelling. That includes the possessive `its`, and a compound that current spelling writes as one word. Hyphenate a compound number, a fraction, and a compound modifier before a noun.

Where an older form now means something else, use the modern word. `ensure` means to make certain, and `insure` means to buy insurance. `Vice President` takes no hyphen. An ordinal uses its modern form.

## Grammar

Use the modern indicative where an older present subjunctive stood for the indicative. Keep a mandative subjunctive that is still ordinary English, as in "that the laws be faithfully executed."

Use `except` for an exception. Use the article that matches the sound that follows it.

Use the serial comma in a list of three or more. Remove a comma that stands only between a subject and its verb. Join independent clauses with a period, a semicolon, or a comma. A relative clause takes a comma. Write `provided that` with no comma before `provided`.

Keep a word the parallel clause already uses.

## What a review leaves in place

A legal term of art keeps its received spelling. That limit overrides the spelling principle above.

Leave a legal term of art as it stands. Leave a legal word that points at an antecedent, including `shall` and words such as `herein`, `thereof`, and `wherein`. Leave an established idiom when replacing it would substitute a different phrase.

Leave `he` and `his` for an officer. The Senate is `they`.

Where folded texts use different forms, leave each form in the text it came from.

Leave headings in the form `Article. I.` and `Section. 1.`
