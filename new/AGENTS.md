# Review of the new constitution

These principles govern newly written text in `new/constitution.md`. Leave `amended/` in its historical form.

Apply them to wording a change adds or rewrites. Report each violation there as severity `bug`. That severity overrides the reviewer persona's rule that a style preference is not a bug. Name the principle the line violates. Wording the change leaves in place is outside this review.

A change of style alone does not get a changelog entry. `CHANGELOG.md` records a change to operative legal meaning. These principles govern form. They do not decide the legal effect of the new text.

## Capitalization

Capitalize the first word of a paragraph or sentence, a proper name, and the name of an office or institution. A name includes a people, a month, and a day. Leave every other noun lowercase.

The principle turns on what the word names:

- A `State` is one of the States, including a foreign State. `state of the union` is a condition.
- A `House` is a chamber of Congress. A `house` is a dwelling.
- The `District` is the seat of government. A judicial `district` is a place of trial. In `seat of the government`, `government` describes the seat.
- `people` is not a proper name.
- `pro tempore` stays lowercase.
- A semicolon or colon does not start a sentence.
- Each enumerated power stays its own paragraph and begins with `To`.

## Spelling

Use current American spelling. That includes the possessive `its`, and a compound that current spelling writes as one word. Hyphenate a compound number, a fraction, and a compound modifier before a noun.

Use the word that has the meaning you intend. `ensure` means to make certain, and `insure` means to buy insurance. `Vice President` takes no hyphen. An ordinal uses its modern form.

## Grammar

Use the modern indicative. Keep a mandative subjunctive that is still ordinary English, as in "that the laws be faithfully executed."

Use `except` for an exception. Use the article that matches the sound that follows it.

Use the serial comma in a list of three or more. Remove a comma that stands only between a subject and its verb. Join independent clauses with a period, a semicolon, or a comma. A relative clause takes a comma. Write `provided that` with no comma before `provided`.

In parallel clauses, repeat the word the parallel clause uses.
