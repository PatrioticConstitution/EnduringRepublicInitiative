# Style of the new constitution

These rules govern `new/constitution.md`. They record the standards of pull requests #37 and #38. Leave `amended/` in its historical form.

When you edit `new/constitution.md`, follow this file. When you review a diff that touches it, report each violation in an added or modified line as severity `bug`. That severity overrides the reviewer persona's rule that a style preference is not a bug. Name the rule that line violates. Leave lines that already follow this file alone.

A change that only applies this file does not get a changelog entry. `CHANGELOG.md` records a change to operative legal meaning.

## Capitalization

Capitalize the first word of a paragraph or sentence, a proper name, and the name of an office or institution. Leave every other noun lowercase. This is how the later amendments are written and how Congress drafts: the House Legislative Counsel's Manual on Drafting Style capitalizes a word only with a reason, and the Government Publishing Office Style Manual capitalizes offices and institutions.

Capitalize:

- `United States`, `America`, `the Constitution`, and `the Confederation`.
- `Congress`, the `Senate`, the `House of Representatives`, and `House` or `Houses` when they mean those chambers.
- `President`, `Vice President`, `President elect`, `Vice President elect`, `Acting President`, `President pro tempore`, and `Commander in Chief`. Keep `pro tempore` lowercase.
- `Senator`, `Representative`, `Speaker`, and `Chief Justice`.
- `State` and `States`, including a foreign `State`. `a free State` keeps `State`.
- the `Supreme Court`.
- the `District`, when it means the seat of government.
- `Treasury`, `Army`, `Navy`, `January`, `Sundays`, and `Indian`.

Leave these lowercase:

- Ordinary nouns, including person, office, right, law, power, term, year, citizen, elector, people, legislature, government, militia, and court. The Supreme Court stays capitalized.
- `We the people`. `People` is not a proper name.
- `seat of the government`. 4 U.S.C. § 71 says "the permanent seat of government of the United States."
- `grand jury` and `militia`.
- `any house`, and `houses, papers, and effects`. Those are dwellings. `each House` is a chamber of Congress.
- `state of the union`, because there it means a condition. A judicial `district` stays lowercase, as in "the State and district."
- The word after a semicolon or colon, unless that word is one of the names above. A semicolon does not start a sentence.
- In the presidential oath, `office` and `ability`.

Each enumerated power stays its own paragraph, so each one still begins with `To`. The lead-in is "shall have power to lay," because that `to` is not the start of a paragraph.

## Spelling

Use current American spelling, the form used in the United States Code. In particular:

- `choose`, `choosing`, `defense`, `behavior`, `increased`, `offense`, `offenses`, `control`.
- `its` for the possessive.
- `ensure` where the sense is to make certain. The preamble says "ensure domestic tranquility." Today `insure` means to buy insurance.
- `3rd`, for the form the Twentieth Amendment printed as `3d`.
- `Vice President`, with no hyphen. That is the Government Publishing Office form.
- Hyphenate compound numbers and fractions: `twenty-five`, `thirty-five`, `one-third`, `one-fifth`, `two-thirds`, `three-fourths`.
- Hyphenate a compound modifier before a noun: `natural-born citizen`, `well-regulated militia`.
- `anything` and `hereinbefore`, each as one word.

## Grammar

Use the modern indicative where an older present subjunctive stood for the indicative: `if he approves`, `before it becomes`, `unless they are equally divided`, `if such number is`, `if no person has`, `before he enters`, `until the disability is removed`.

Keep the mandative subjunctive in "take care that the laws be faithfully executed."

Keep a word the parallel clause already uses: "nor shall any State be formed," and "a State shall be a party."

Use `except` where the sense is an exception, including money drawn from the Treasury, warrants, and quartering soldiers in wartime.

Use `a uniform`, because `uniform` begins with a consonant sound.

Remove a comma that stands only between a subject and its verb. Use the serial comma in a list of three or more. Join two independent clauses with a period, a semicolon, or a comma. A relative clause such as "which day shall be the same" takes a comma before it. Write `provided that` with no comma before `provided`.

## Keep

- `shall`.
- `he` and `his` for the President and for other officers. The Senate stays `they`, because the Senate is plural.
- `herein`, `thereof`, `therein`, `whereof`, and `wherein`.
- `more perfect`, `necessary to a choice`, `fill up`, and `yeas and nays`.
- `legislature` in Article I, section 2, and `legislatures` in section 3.
- `dock-yards`, `ex post facto`, `habeas corpus`, `bill of attainder`, `marque and reprisal`, and `pro tempore`.
- Headings in the form `Article. I.` and `Section. 1.`
- Operative legal meaning. These rules add or remove no institution, office, power, or right.
